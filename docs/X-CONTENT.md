# Posting on X — the idea

_Draft for review. Nothing here is built or running. Opened 29 Aug 2026._

## What I'd be posting

**Second opinions on the tape.** When a company I cover reports, or files
something, I read the actual document and post what's in it that the threads
missed or got wrong.

Not "here's what happened" — everyone does that within minutes. The angle is:
**I read the filing, they read each other.**

## Why this can work

The desk already does it every week. Recent examples, all from the verdicts:

- **NVIDIA** — everyone covered the earnings beat. Almost nobody covered the
  17 August filing where NVIDIA backstopped up to $105bn of residual value on
  a data centre its largest customer leases.
- **Applied Optoelectronics** — the demand story was everywhere. The share
  count going from 62.4m to 84.6m in twelve months was not.
- **Alphabet** — record cloud demand was the headline. Free cash flow being
  negative because capex now exceeds operating cash flow was not.

That is a real edge and it does not depend on anyone else's ideas.

## The rule that keeps me out of trouble

> **Facts from companies. Interpretation from my desk. Nothing from his feed.**

My raw input is one identifiable person's posts. If I publish analysis built
on his reasoning, I'm monetising his work, and that is both a legal and a
reputation problem.

So everything I post must trace to a public document — a filing, an earnings
release, a transcript — or to something my own desk computed. His name and his
private reasoning stay out entirely.

This is not a limitation. The desk's own measurements are the most original
thing I have.

## When I post

Event-driven, never on a schedule. A post needs one of:

1. A company I cover reported, **and I read the release** — not the thread
2. A filing landed with something material nobody is discussing
3. A number in wide circulation is checkably wrong

No event, no post. Busy in earnings season, quiet otherwise. That is fine.

## Before pressing post — the checklist

1. Did I read the primary document, or someone's summary of it?
2. Can every number be pointed at a filing, release or transcript?
3. Is anything here from his feed rather than a company? If yes, cut it.
4. What would make me wrong — and did I say it?
5. Disclaimer on.

## Example post

> Most people describing NVIDIA's $105B OpenAI commitment have it slightly
> wrong, and the difference is the whole story.
>
> NVIDIA did **not** guarantee OpenAI's rent.
>
> It guaranteed the *residual value*. Filed 17 Aug, Item 2.03. If OpenAI
> defaults, SB Energy first re-leases or sells the site — NVIDIA only covers
> what's left short of a guaranteed minimum.
>
> That's not $105B of rent exposure. It's an asset-value backstop on ~4.25GW
> at the old Portsmouth plant in Ohio, on a 20-year lease.
>
> Still remarkable: a chip company underwriting the asset risk of its largest
> customer's landlord, with another 3.8GW available at its own discretion.
>
> But "NVIDIA is liable for $105B" is wrong, and the filing says so plainly.
>
> _Not investment advice._

Checkable, sourced to a public filing, corrects a widely repeated error, and
contains nothing from anyone else's feed.

## What I decided against, and why

- **A fixed weekly cadence** — I'm job-searching. A quota produces filler, and
  filler in this lane is worse than silence.
- **Being "the AI semis take guy"** — the most crowded niche on finance X, and
  the person I follow has ~1M followers in it. Competing there with his own
  ideas as raw material is a fight I lose and it looks derivative.
- **Auto-posting** — Claude drafts, I press post. Publishing is public and
  irreversible; that stays a human button.

## If I go ahead

Add a step to `/weekly-review` that spots post-worthy events — a covered name
reported, a filing landed with something material — and **drafts** the post
with its sources attached. It arrives as a draft. I decide if it ever leaves
the machine.

## Open questions for me to answer

1. Am I willing to be publicly wrong? The whole method depends on posting a
   falsifier and then honouring it.
2. Do I want to be known for this, given the job search?
3. Is there a version where I credit the analyst openly instead of avoiding
   him? That's a different, friendlier product — and a conversation with him,
   not a decision I make alone.
